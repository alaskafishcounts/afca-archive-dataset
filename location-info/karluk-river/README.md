# 🐟 Karluk River - Historical Fish Count Data

**Location ID 24: Karluk River, Kodiak Island, Alaska**

The Karluk River represents the earliest systematic fish counting in Alaska, with historical data spanning 71 years from 1882 to 1952. This location provides crucial baseline information for understanding long-term salmon population trends and environmental changes in Alaska's commercial fishing history.

## 📊 Location Overview

- **Location ID**: 24
- **Location Name**: Karluk River
- **Region**: Kodiak Island, Alaska
- **Coordinates**: 57.4°N, 154.1°W
- **Data Period**: 1882-1952 (71 years)
- **Data Type**: Historical year-total catch records
- **Primary Species**: Sockeye Salmon (Oncorhynchus nerka)

## 🏔️ Historical Context

The Karluk River sockeye salmon data represents the earliest systematic fish counting in Alaska, beginning in 1882. This historical data comes directly from the National Archives Record Group 022 document titled "Catch of Karluk River Red Salmon from beginning of the canning industry in 1882 to 1952" and provides crucial baseline information for understanding long-term salmon population trends and environmental changes over 71 years.

### Primary Source Document:
- **Document**: National Archives Record Group 022
- **Title**: "Catch of Karluk River Red Salmon from beginning of the canning industry in 1882 to 1952"
- **Archive URL**: [https://catalog.archives.gov/id/312419233?objectPage=9](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/312419233?objectPage=9)
- **Image**: [Direct document image](https://s3.amazonaws.com/NARAprodstorage/lz/seattle/rg-022/95115924/Batch0003/95115924-073/95115924-073-011/95115924-073-011-0002.jpg)

## 📈 Key Historical Periods

### 1882-1900: Early Commercial Fishing Era
- **Peak Year**: 1901 with 3,985,177 fish
- **Characteristics**: Rapid expansion of commercial fishing
- **Average Catch**: ~1.5 million fish per year
- **Significance**: Establishment of commercial fishing industry

### 1900-1920: Transition to Conservation Management
- **Peak Year**: 1916 with 2,343,104 fish
- **Characteristics**: Introduction of conservation measures
- **Average Catch**: ~1.2 million fish per year
- **Significance**: Early fisheries management efforts

### 1920-1940: Pre-war Fishing Expansion
- **Peak Year**: 1926 with 2,386,335 fish
- **Characteristics**: Technological improvements in fishing
- **Average Catch**: ~1.0 million fish per year
- **Significance**: Peak of historical fishing operations

### 1940-1952: Post-war Fishing Development
- **Final Year**: 1952 with 218,791 fish
- **Characteristics**: Declining catches, modern management
- **Average Catch**: ~400,000 fish per year
- **Significance**: Transition to modern ADF&G management

## 🔬 Data Quality & Methodology

### Data Collection Method
- **Source**: Commercial catch records from canning industry
- **Method**: Weir operations and commercial catch data
- **Accuracy**: Single annual totals (no daily breakdown)
- **Reliability**: High - official commercial records

### Data Limitations
- **Temporal Resolution**: Annual totals only
- **Species Coverage**: Sockeye salmon only
- **Geographic Scope**: Single location (Karluk River)
- **Method Changes**: Commercial fishing methods evolved over time
